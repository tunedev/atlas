# Epic 7 — Fit judgement — design

**Supersedes nothing.** This design details Epic 7 of `docs/plans/2026-09-17-roadmap.md`. It
rests on Epic 3's `Judge` (`docs/design/the-judge.md`), Epic 5's `Source` and feed
(`docs/specs/2026-09-24-epic-5-feed.md`), and Epic 6's deal-breakers and decision log
(`docs/specs/2026-09-24-epic-6-profile.md`). It assumes PR #24 has landed: the seniority fix
(`staff` to `principal`) and the laya spike note it carries are referenced below.

**Goal:** one run scores a whole board of roles against the profile. Each role gets an
apply, stretch or skip verdict, with reasons a person can read and disagree with. Every tripped
deal-breaker is named, and an override is recorded as data.

## Changed during implementation

- **`reach` replaces `stretch`** in the verdict's options: `stretch` and `skip` share an
  initial, which `AnswerSchema` rejects.
- **A judged rule requires `ask`.** Measured on real roles: a question derived from
  `Statement` alone tripped on 0 of 20 true positives, against 15 of 20 for asking the
  condition directly.
- **Subject ids use `[[ replace .item.id "/" ":" ]]`**, because a subject id may not contain
  `/`.
- **The reuse key is subject id plus fingerprint**, and the fingerprint covers what the judge
  sends (the system message, the user message, and the schema), the rule inputs, and the
  verdict question's id.
- **The answer schema is sent in declared order**, proven by the apply-heavy regression this
  fix corrects (`docs/notes/2026-09-27-epic-7.md`).
- **No rebuild extractor exists** for judgements, so the index's `fingerprint`, `verdict` and
  `tripped` fields are derivable from the document but are not yet rebuilt from it.
- **An undecodable item aborts the run**, fail-closed the same way `source.pull` is.

## Evidence this design rests on

Measured before designing, as the roadmap's own "one unknown per increment" asks. A throwaway
probe ran `app.Judge` against real roles: 20 from GitLab (Greenhouse) and 20 from Palantir
(Lever), each asked on behalf of the job-hunt pack's candidate. Engine: Ollama 0.23,
`qwen2.5-coder:7b` (Q4_K_M) on an 8 GB laptop GPU, temperature 0, seed 1, five alternatives
per token. Nothing here is estimated; every figure is a measured run.

### Choice questions fail because the prompt never names their options

| Question | Options named in the question text? | Coverage | Confidence | Calls lost to errors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `focus` [backend, frontend, platform, data, other] | no | 0.00 (40 of 40 zero) | 1.00 | 0 of 40 |
| `focus`, two other option vocabularies | no | 0.00 to 0.01 | 1.00 | 9 and 20 of 40 |
| `focus` [backend, frontend, platform, data, other] | **yes** | **0.81** | 0.58 | 0 of 40 |
| `focus` [engineering, product, sales, support, operations, other] | **yes** | **0.59** | 0.76 | 0 of 40 |
| `seniority` [junior, mid, senior, staff] | no | 0.71 | 0.82 | **6 of 40** |
| `seniority` [junior, mid, senior, principal] | no | 0.60 | 0.86 | 0 of 40 |

`app.promptUserMessage` writes each question as `id: ask`. The options reach the engine only
as a JSON Schema grammar. The alternatives mass is read from are the model's distribution
before the grammar applies, so a model that has never been told the options answers in its
own words. At the `focus` answer position those were sentence starts: `develop`, `building`,
`leading`, `technical`. None is an option, so coverage is 0, and the grammar then forces a
legal answer whose "confidence" of 1.00 measures nothing.

Changing the option vocabulary does not help. Naming the options does. That settles the
question the laya spike opened: a purpose-built classifier is not needed to fix this failure,
because the failure was the judge's own prompt. The classifier stays a validated option on
record (`docs/notes/2026-09-24-laya-spike.md`). Its Go binding is still untagged upstream.

The `staff` errors come from a separate, known fault: two options sharing a first character
(`s`) that the engine emits as its own token. A single error loses every answer in that call.

### The verdict must be a choice, not a yes/no

Asked as a three-way choice, "Should the candidate apply, treat it as a stretch, or skip
it?", the verdict reached coverage 0.95 and tracked the role titles sensibly. It skipped a
Director of Social Media and an International Tax Manager at 1.00, and put a Senior Backend
Engineer (Go) at apply. Asked as a yes/no, "Should the candidate apply?", it said **yes** to
both of those non-engineering roles (0.61 and 0.55) for a Go backend candidate. A yes/no fit
question carries a yes bias. The verdict is a choice.

A yes/no deal-breaker question worked well: "Does this role involve on-call or a pager
rotation?" reached coverage 0.96 to 1.00.

### What scoring a board costs

| Configuration | Result |
|---|---|
| One role per call, full description, 4 to 5 questions | 2.7 s per call; 22 roles/min; **200 roles in about 9 minutes** |
| Breakdown of one call | prompt evaluation 0.6 to 1.0 s (1.4k to 2.7k tokens); about 30 answer tokens at 20 ms; about 0.85 s to return per-token alternatives |
| 2 and 4 calls in flight | identical 22 roles/min: this Ollama serves one request at a time |
| 5 roles in one call (descriptions cut to 1500 chars to fit) | 23.5 s against 20.1 s for the same 20 roles one by one; 34 of 40 answers agree with asking each alone |
| 10 roles in one call (cut to 700 chars) | 22.4 s against 20.1 s; 28 of 40 agree |
| Descriptions cut to 1500 chars, one role per call | 20% faster (441 prompt tokens against 1652), but `on_call` flipped on 9 of 20 roles |

The time is model compute, not round trips. Putting several roles in one prompt saves
nothing and changes the answers. Shortening descriptions saves little and changes them more.
Story 7.4's acceptance is therefore reworded (below): one run scores a board, with one model
call per new or changed role.

Two operational facts surfaced as well:
- **Silent truncation.** Ollama loaded the model with a 4096-token context and truncates
  anything longer without saying so. The longest role seen was 2.7k tokens, under the limit,
  but nothing guarantees that.
- **Load can wedge the engine.** The probe's load ended with the Ollama runner stuck in the
  kernel and `nvidia-smi` hanging, which took a reboot to clear. Running calls one after
  another, as this design does, is also the load this laptop tolerated.

## The stories, as this epic delivers them

| Story | Delivered as |
|---|---|
| 7.1 Apply, stretch or skip, with reasons | The pack's `verdict` choice question, plus reasons built from the same call's typed answers |
| 7.2 A tripped deal-breaker is named | Each rule reports tripped, clear or unknown, with its evidence, listed first among the reasons |
| 7.3 Disagreement is recorded | A decision's index row says whether the choice agreed with the verdict it overrode |
| 7.4 Scoring a board is one run | One `judge.each` step scores every matching role; a role already judged with the same inputs is reused, not re-asked |

The roadmap's 7.4 acceptance, "two hundred postings scored without two hundred round trips",
is replaced by: **"one command scores a board; a role whose judged inputs have not changed is
never judged twice."** The measurements above are the reason.

## 1. The judge names every question's options

`app.promptUserMessage` writes each question as:

```
seniority: How senior is this role? (one of: junior, mid, senior, principal)
on_call: Does this role involve on-call or a pager rotation? (one of: yes, no)
```

A yes/no question with no declared options lists `yes, no`, the canonical forms
`ports.NoulOptions()` already defines. Only the declared options are listed, not the extra
surface forms (`Forms`) that count toward them.

This is a change at the source. It fixes the fault for `judge.ask`, for `judge.each`, and for
every future caller at once. No pack has to learn to list its options inside `ask`. Naming
options in the prompt made a live engine more likely to emit a shared-first-character
truncation as a low-probability alternative; `AnswerSchema` now rejects any option set sharing
a first character before the provider is ever called, so that truncation can no longer reach a
live call at all.

**The judge also refuses a prompt that may have been truncated.** When a completion reports
prompt tokens at or above `JudgeConfig.ContextTokens` (config, default 4096, the engine's
measured window), `Judge.Ask` returns an error, "prompt may be truncated", instead of a
judgement. Only `app.Judge` sees the completion's usage, so this is where the check can live.
It protects `judge.ask` and `judge.each` alike. Raising the engine's window is an operator
change to the engine, not to atlas.

## 2. `judge.each` — one run over a board

A new generic tool in `internal/adapters/outbound/tools`, composed from ports that already
exist: `Source`, `Judge`, `Docs` and `Index`. It adds no port and changes no port.

### What a pack writes

```yaml
- id: board
  tool: judge.each
  with:
    prefix: "postings/greenhouse/{{ .vars.board }}"
    match: status=open
    subject_id: '[[ replace .item.id "/" ":" ]]'
    subject: |
      Candidate: {{ .vars.profile }}

      Role: [[ .item.title ]] at [[ .item.company ]]

      [[ .item.description_text ]]
    verdict: verdict
    questions: |
      - id: verdict
        type: choice
        options: [apply, reach, skip]
        ask: Should the candidate apply, treat it as a reach, or skip it?
      - id: seniority
        type: score
        levels: [junior, mid, senior, principal]
        ask: How senior is this role?
    rules: profile/dealbreakers.json
    rule_fields: |
      pay-floor: compensation.min
```

- `prefix` and `match` select items exactly as `source.pull` does.
- `subject_id` and `subject` are per-item templates. The runner renders `{{ }}` once, before the tool runs, as
  it does for every step. The tool then renders `[[ ]]`, once per item, with `.item` bound to
  that item's decoded document. Missing keys fail, as `{{ }}` rendering already does.
- `verdict` names the question whose answer is the verdict. `questions` uses `judge.ask`'s
  YAML shape and parser unchanged.
- `rules` is a path in `Docs`: Epic 6's deal-breakers document. `rule_fields` maps a numeric
  rule's id to a dotted path in the item. Both are optional.

### What one run does

For each selected item, in id order and one call at a time:

1. **Render** the subject.
2. **Fingerprint** it: a SHA-256 over the rendered subject, the question set, the rules as
   loaded, and the model name.
3. **Reuse** when the index already holds a judgement with that fingerprint: return that
   judgement's result with `reused: true`, making no model call.
4. **Ask** otherwise: one `Judge.Ask` with the pack's questions plus one yes/no question per
   wording rule (section 3).
5. **Check** numeric rules in code, against the item's fields (section 3).
6. **Record** the judgement with its fingerprint and rule results (section 5).
7. **Report** one row.

### What it returns

```json
{
  "rows": [
    {
      "subject_id": "greenhouse:gitlab:8556658002",
      "verdict": "apply", "p": 0.55,
      "reasons": [
        "on-call tripped: p(yes) 0.71 >= 0.6",
        "pay-floor unknown: compensation.min not in the role",
        "seniority: senior (0.82)"
      ],
      "rules": [{"id": "on-call", "state": "tripped", "evidence": "p(yes) 0.71 >= 0.6"}],
      "judgement_path": "judgements/greenhouse:gitlab:8556658002/<ts>.json",
      "reused": false
    }
  ],
  "_meta": {"count": 187, "judged": 12, "reused": 175, "errors": 0, "fetched_at": "...", "stale": false}
}
```

`_meta` carries the source's freshness exactly as `source.pull` reports it, and warns the same
way when the source is stale.

### Failure, bounds and truncation

- **One role failing never stops the board.** A failed role becomes a row carrying `error`
  instead of a verdict. The tool errors only when every selected role failed, or when a
  record cannot be written: a board whose results cannot be kept is not a partial success.
- **Calls run one after another.** Measured concurrency bought nothing on this engine, and
  sustained parallel load is what wedged it.
- **Every call keeps the judge's existing timeout.** A board of new roles takes minutes; the
  tool is bounded by the runner's context, not by an extra deadline of its own.
- **A possibly truncated prompt is refused** by the judge (section 1), and that role becomes
  an error row.

## 3. Rules

Epic 6 stored deal-breakers as data, and classified each kind, without evaluating them. This
epic evaluates them. In the Go tree they are **rules**: `vocabulary_test.go` forbids
`deal-breaker` and its spellings there, and nothing about evaluating a threshold is specific
to a job hunt.

```go
// Rule is one condition a subject must not meet. Kind "comparable" is
// checked in code against a field of the subject's document; kind "judged"
// is asked of the Judge as a yes/no question, and trips when p(yes)
// reaches Threshold.
type Rule struct {
    ID        string
    Statement string
    Kind      string   // "comparable" or "judged"
    Op        string   // comparable: ">=", "<=", ">", "<", "==", "!="
    Value     any      // comparable: a number, or a string for "==" and "!="
    Threshold float64  // judged: default 0.5
    Ask       string   // judged: required, the yes/no question whose yes means the rule trips
}

// RuleResult is what checking one rule found. State is "tripped",
// "clear" or "unknown"; Evidence says why, in words a person can check.
type RuleResult struct {
    ID       string
    State    string
    Evidence string
}
```

- **A numeric rule** reads its field from the item through the pack's `rule_fields` path. For
  a salary floor, `>=` means "must be at least". A rule trips when the comparison fails, and
  evidence quotes the value found and the line it missed.
- **A missing field, or one of the wrong type, is `unknown`.** Unknown is listed as a reason
  and never trips. The feed's schema v1 carries no pay, so a pay floor reads `unknown` for
  every role until the feed carries compensation. That is a feed change for a later epic.
  A model is never asked to supply a number a posting may not state.
- **A wording rule** becomes one yes/no question in the same `Judge.Ask` call, with id
  `rule_<id>`. It trips when the answer's p(yes) reaches the rule's threshold. Evidence gives
  the probability and the threshold.
- **How a wording rule is phrased as a question is measured before it is built.** Epic 6's
  rules carry only a `statement` ("No on-call or pager rotation."). The derived question the
  plan will test is "Does this role break this rule: <statement>?". The measured question
  that worked asked about the condition directly ("Does this role involve on-call or a pager
  rotation?"). The plan's first task measures both phrasings on real roles. If the derived one
  loses, rules gain an optional `ask` field that states the condition as a question, and
  `statement` stays the human-readable line.

## 4. Verdict and reasons

- **The verdict is the model's.** It is the `Chosen` answer to the question the pack names in
  `verdict`, with that answer's probability. A tripped rule does not change it. This is the
  user's explicit call: rules flag, they do not override.
- **Reasons are the same call's evidence,** with no second model call and no free prose. In
  order:
  1. tripped rules
  2. unknown rules
  3. every other question's answer, with its probability and, when coverage is thin, that
     fact: `focus: data (1.00, coverage 0 of 5)`
  4. clear rules

  Every line points at something recorded, so a person can disagree with a specific line
  rather than with a paragraph.
- **Ordering and filtering the board belong to the pack or the reader.** The tool reports
  every role, in id order. It does not rank, and does not hide skips.

## 5. The record

- **The judgement document** gains `fingerprint` and `rules` (the list of rule results),
  written through the existing `RecordJudgement` path. `judge.ask` writes neither and is
  otherwise unchanged.
- **The judgement's index row** gains three fields:
  - `fingerprint`, which is what `judge.each` looks a judgement up by;
  - `verdict`, the verdict question's chosen answer;
  - `tripped`, the ids of the tripped rules, comma-separated.

  A reader can then list "every role I was told to apply to that tripped nothing" from the
  index alone.
- **Rebuilding the index** from `Docs` is, for every other kind, how the derived index is
  restored from the record. No rebuild extractor exists yet for a judgement's `fingerprint`,
  `verdict` and `tripped` fields: they are derivable from the document, the same as any other
  kind's fields, but a rebuild does not yet restore them (see "Changed during implementation").

## 6. Disagreement (7.3)

Epic 6's `decision.record` already snapshots `VerdictAtDecision` from the judgement it
points at. This epic adds one index field to the decision row: `agrees`, which is `"true"`
when the choice equals the verdict at decision time, `"false"` when it does not, and absent
when no judgement preceded the decision.

Every override is then one index query. No new document shape and no new tool are needed. The
verdict question's options (`apply`, `reach`, `skip`) and the decision's choices share
words: choosing `apply` on a `reach` verdict reads as a disagreement, which is exactly what
it is.

## 7. The pack

`packs/job-hunt.yaml` becomes board scoring: one `judge.each` step replaces the
`role`/`judged`/`verdict`/`documents` sequence that judged a single role. The one-role
drafting step leaves the pack, since drafting documents is Epic 9's. `packs/decide.yaml`'s
usage comment points at a judgement from a board run and uses `verdict_question: verdict`.

## Testing

- **Offline, the judge prompt:** every question's options appear in the prompt, and a yes/no
  question with no options lists `yes, no`. A mutation dropping the options fails it.
- **Offline, `judge.each`,** against an in-memory `Source`, a scripted `Judge` and the real
  `gitdocs` and `sqlindex` over temporary directories:
  - rows in id order;
  - a second run over unchanged items reuses every judgement and makes no call;
  - changing one item's text re-judges only that item;
  - one role failing yields an error row while the rest complete;
  - every role failing errors.
- **Offline, truncation:** a scripted completion reporting prompt tokens at the limit makes
  `Judge.Ask` error; one token under does not.
- **Offline, rules:** each operator, a missing field (unknown), a wrong type (unknown), and a
  judged rule at, just under and above its threshold.
- **Offline, `agrees`** on a decision row: equal, different, and no judgement.
- **Live, skipped unless `ATLAS_LIVE_PROVIDER` is set:** a choice question's coverage is not
  zero on a real engine. This is the regression the whole first section exists for.
- **Vocabulary:** every Go test uses neutral subjects (books, weather), as the architecture
  test requires.

## Deliberately not in this increment

| Out | Why |
|---|---|
| Several roles per model call | Measured: no faster, and 15 to 30% of answers change |
| Parallel calls | Measured: no throughput gain on this engine; sustained load wedged it |
| Truncating descriptions to save time | Measured: 20% faster, and deal-breaker answers flip |
| A purpose-built classifier as a second `Judge` | Not needed for the failure measured; its Go binding is still untagged |
| Free-text reasons | The typed answers are the reasons; prose would be ungrounded and double the cost |
| Rules overriding the verdict | The user's decision: rules flag, the verdict stays the model's |
| Pay in the feed | A feed schema change for a later epic; until then a pay floor reads unknown |
| Drafting documents for a role | Epic 9 |
| Ranking the board | A reader's or later pack's concern; the tool reports every role in id order |
| Acting on thin coverage or low confidence | Recorded and shown in reasons; acting on it belongs to Epic 11 |

## Open questions

- **Whether the feed workflow's own commits keep its schedule alive** is Epic 5's open
  question and unchanged here. A stale feed is reported by `judge.each` exactly as by
  `source.pull`.
- **Calibration.** Nothing yet checks that a verdict at 0.8 is right more often than one at
  0.5. That needs outcomes, which is Epic 11.
- **A second engine.** Every figure above is one engine and one model. Whether vLLM, or a
  larger model, changes the verdict's yes bias or the cost is unmeasured.
